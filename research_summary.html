Intelligent and Secure Autonomous Vehicle Platooning

 

Autonomous vehicle platooning requires the seamless integration of communication, sensing, artificial intelligence, and control to ensure safe, efficient, and resilient transportation systems. This research focuses on the development of intelligent platoon networks that combine software-defined radio (SDR)-based vehicle-to-vehicle (V2V) communication, machine learning-driven wireless security, and reinforcement learning-based autonomous decision making. The developed frameworks enable coordinated multi-vehicle movement, dynamic platoon formation and reconfiguration, real-time obstacle avoidance, and adaptive response to wireless threats such as radio-frequency jamming. Using Raspberry Pi platforms, USRP software-defined radios, LiDAR, ultrasonic sensors, and machine learning algorithms, the proposed systems demonstrate reliable communication, resilient platoon operation, and autonomous control in dynamic environments. Together, these studies contribute toward the realization of secure, AI-enabled, and next-generation intelligent transportation systems capable of maintaining robust operation under both physical and cyber-physical challenges.

Physics-Informed Neural Networks for Quantum Systems

 

Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) provide a powerful framework for solving quantum mechanical problems by embedding the governing laws of physics directly into deep learning models. This research investigates the application of PINNs to both time-independent and time-dependent Schrödinger equations, enabling accurate reconstruction of quantum wavefunctions and quantum state evolution under challenging conditions such as noisy observations, incomplete measurements, and unknown system parameters. The developed methods successfully model multiple quantum states of the quantum harmonic oscillator while maintaining consistency with the underlying physical laws. By combining data-driven learning with physics-based constraints, these approaches offer an efficient alternative to traditional numerical solvers and demonstrate significant potential for quantum computing, quantum communication, quantum system identification, and scientific machine learning applications.

Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC) for Intelligent Transportation Systems

 

Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC) is emerging as a key technology for future 6G networks by enabling wireless systems to perform sensing and communication using shared hardware, spectrum, and signal resources. This research investigates MIMO-OFDM-based ISAC frameworks for intelligent transportation systems, focusing on vehicle detection, speed estimation, collision avoidance, and secure wireless communication. By combining phased-array beamforming, OFDM waveform design, channel estimation, and Doppler-based sensing, the proposed systems simultaneously provide high-resolution environmental awareness and reliable data transmission. The developed frameworks demonstrate accurate target localization, velocity estimation, range-Doppler analysis, and robust 64-QAM communication performance under realistic multipath and cluttered environments. These capabilities establish ISAC as a promising solution for autonomous driving, vehicle-to-everything (V2X) communications, transportation safety, and security-aware wireless networks.

Quantum Optimization for Wireless Networks

 

Wireless networks often involve complex optimization problems such as channel assignment, interference mitigation, routing, scheduling, and resource allocation, many of which are computationally intractable using conventional approaches. This research investigates the application of quantum-inspired optimization techniques to wireless communication systems by modeling interference-aware resource allocation problems as combinatorial optimization tasks. Specifically, wireless mesh network interference management is formulated as a weighted Max-Cut problem, where network nodes are represented as graph vertices and interference relationships are modeled as weighted edges. The Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) is employed to identify near-optimal network partitions that reduce intra-group interference and improve spectrum utilization. By combining graph-theoretic modeling, Ising Hamiltonian representations, and hybrid quantum-classical optimization, the proposed framework demonstrates the potential of quantum computing for solving large-scale wireless network optimization problems and lays the foundation for future quantum-assisted 5G/6G communication systems.

  1. I. Mahmud, and A. Abdelhadi, “Optimization of Interference-Aware Wireless Mesh Networks Using Quantum Algorithm,” IEEE International Conference on Quantum Communications, Networking, and Computing (QCNC) 2026. (IEEE Xplore)

Artificial Intelligence and Quantum Technologies

 

Artificial Intelligence (AI) and quantum technologies are rapidly converging to address fundamental challenges in secure communications, fault-tolerant computing, network optimization, and large-scale quantum information processing. This research direction provides comprehensive analyses of emerging methodologies at the intersection of AI, quantum communication, and quantum error correction. The surveyed works examine the application of machine learning techniques for quantum communication tasks such as error correction, quantum key distribution, network optimization, entanglement management, and resource allocation, while also exploring the broader landscape of quantum coding strategies designed to mitigate decoherence, noise, and operational errors. By systematically comparing existing approaches, identifying open challenges, and outlining future research opportunities, these studies serve as foundational references for researchers developing scalable, secure, and intelligent quantum computing and communication systems.

Digital Twin Assisted Wireless Propagation Modeling and Ray Tracing

 

Wireless signal propagation in complex urban environments is influenced by numerous factors including building geometry, multipath reflections, diffraction, propagation delay, path loss, and phase variations. This research investigates the integration of deterministic ray tracing and machine learning within realistic digital twin environments to model and predict wireless network performance. Using three-dimensional city-scale digital twins generated from OpenStreetMap data and MATLAB-based ray tracing simulations, large-scale datasets are created to capture diverse propagation characteristics under realistic deployment scenarios. Multiple machine learning models, including Neural Networks, Random Forests, Gradient Boosting, Support Vector Machines, and K-Nearest Neighbors, are evaluated for received power prediction and wireless channel approximation. The developed frameworks demonstrate that AI-driven models can accurately learn complex propagation behaviors while significantly reducing the computational cost associated with repeated ray tracing simulations. These studies provide a foundation for intelligent wireless network planning, digital twin-assisted optimization, and future AI-native 5G/6G communication systems.

Qunatum-Based Algorithm for Multi-Hop Relay Path Selection Using Fade Duration

 

Cooperative multi-hop relaying is a fundamental strategy for extending coverage in 6G networks, and relay path selection directly influences end-to-end reliability. Conventional approaches use the signal-to-noise ratio as the link-quality parameter and either a greedy or an exhaustive search as the solver. However, SNR only measures average channel strength and ignores the temporal duration of fade events, which determines burst-error performance in delay-sensitive traffic. This study presents a relay path selection system based on the quantum approximate optimization algorithm (QAOA), with average fade duration (AFD) as the edge metric. The problem is expressed as a quadratic unconstrained binary optimization, which is addressed through a depth-two QAOA circuit over an eight-qubit two-tier decode-and-forward (DF) relay network. Simulation results show that QAOA-AFD closely follows the optimum exhaustive search approach and reduces total path AFD by up to 37% compared to conventional SNR-based selection. The comparison analysis also demonstrates that the choice of edge metric, not solver quality, is the most important factor in path performance.

  1. A.-M. Zakari Adams, A. R. Hussein, S. Vishwanath, E. Hossain, Z. Han, and A. Abdelhadi, “Quantum-Based Multi-Hop Relay Path Selection Algorithm Using Fade Duration,” IEEE Cyber Awareness and Research Symposium (CARS) 2026. (accepted)

Password Recovery using Partitioned Grover's Quantum Algorithm

 

Grover's algorithm provides a quadratic speedup over classical brute-force search and poses a credible threat to password-based authentication systems, including bcrypt, PBKDF2, and Argon2. Direct application to realistic password lengths is, however, limited by existing NISQ hardware: restricted qubit counts and short coherence durations make full-circuit execution unfeasible. This paper describes the first actual Qiskit implementation of a partitioned Grover framework, which splits the password into two parts: a quantum part searched by Grover's algorithm and a classical part enumerated by the coordinator. A formal threshold-based detection criterion separates correct and incorrect classical partitions. The framework is expanded to partition sizes of m = 3, 4, 5, and 6 qubits, and the simulated success probability is within 0.001 of the theoretical prediction for all tested sizes. Under a fixed five-qubit hardware budget, the partitioned circuit can handle passwords up to seven bits long with a steady success probability, but the regular Grover circuit becomes infeasible for n > 5. Each extra qubit reduces the classical computational burden exponentially, but quantum work grows only sub-linearly.

  1. A.-M. Zakari Adams and A. Abdelhadi, “Password Recovery using Partitioned Grover's Algorithm,” IEEE CARS 2026. (accepted)

RSMA-Enabled RIS-Assisted Integrated Sensing and Communication for 6G

 

Next-generation wireless communication systems are expected to handle sensing and communication on a shared hardware platform jointly. This survey examines the integration of rate-splitting multiple access (RSMA), reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS), and integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) for 6G. RSMA offers a flexible interference management framework, RIS introduces a programmable wireless propagation environment, and ISAC enables simultaneous data transmission and environmental sensing. The survey covers fundamental principles, mathematical models, and performance metrics for RSMA-RIS-ISAC systems. A comparative analysis of 20 recent works demonstrates that RSMA consistently outperforms NOMA and SDMA in spectral efficiency, energy efficiency, and sensing accuracy. The role of artificial intelligence and machine learning in addressing the non-convex optimization challenges of these systems is also examined, along with open research directions toward practical 6G deployment.

Semantic Communication for Reliable and Secure Wireless Image Transmission

 

The explosive growth of image traffic in social media, the Internet of Things, and autonomous systems has made reliable image delivery a central challenge of next-generation wireless networks. Conventional systems pursue a deceptively simple goal — reproduce every transmitted bit — which serves us well on benign channels but degrades sharply under the noise, deep fading, and intentional jamming of real radio environments, collapsing into the familiar cliff effect once the error rate crosses a threshold. The premise of this research line is that, for images, perfect bit recovery is the wrong objective: what a human observer needs is the meaning of the image — its edges, shapes, and structure — not the exact value of every pixel. We therefore design a deep learning-based semantic communication system whose encoder, built from convolutional and residual layers, learns to compress the semantic essence of an image, while a Vision Transformer (ViT) decoder reconstructs global structure and detail at the receiver. Because the latent representation carries meaning rather than literal bits, a perceptually accurate image can be recovered even when many physical-layer symbols are corrupted. We further pair this codec with Alamouti space-time block coding and its OSTBC and OSFBC variants — finding that the better mapping is the one aligned with the more stable channel dimension — and validate the full pipeline on a real LTE-based MU-MIMO testbed using USRP software-defined radios. Across simulation and over-the-air experiments under noise, Rayleigh fading, and deliberate jamming, the proposed models consistently achieve higher structural similarity index (SSIM) and lower mean squared error (MSE) than 256-QAM and 64-QAM baselines, with the largest gains in the low-SNR, adversarial regime.

  1. S. R. Doha and A. Abdelhadi, “Image Transmission with Semantic Communication using Alamouti Scheme,” 2024 International Conference on Computer and Applications (ICCA) 2024. (IEEE Xplore)

  2. S. R. Doha, I. Mahmud, and A. Abdelhadi, “SDR Implementation of MU-MIMO Semantic Communication under Jamming Attacks,” IEEE CARS 2025. (IEEE Xplore)

  3. S. R. Doha and A. Abdelhadi, “Resilient Semantic Image Delivery Under Jamming Attacks: A Comparative Study of OSTBC and OSFBC,” IEEE CARS 2025. (IEEE Xplore)

Artificial Intelligence in Wireless Communication

 

Traditional wireless receivers are engineered as a chain of hand-crafted, model-based blocks — synchronization, channel estimation, equalization, demodulation, decoding — each derived from elegant but rigid assumptions that strain under the dynamic, nonlinear channels of modern networks. Deep learning offers a different proposition: rather than encoding our assumptions into fixed algorithms, we let the receiver learn the mapping from received to transmitted signals directly from data and adapt as conditions change. This research direction surveys, organizes, and critically examines how that shift is playing out across the wireless stack. The first survey takes a receiver-centric view, walking through the receiver chain module by module and matching each stage to the deep learning architecture best suited to it — mapping MLPs, CNNs, RNNs, GANs, and autoencoders onto the tasks they serve best — before broadening to semantic and task-oriented receivers, OFDM and MIMO, Next-G and high-mobility links, and learned interference cancellation. The second survey addresses the gap between simulation and real hardware, using software-defined radio (SDR) as the bridge to validate learning-based methods over the air. Tracing a path from classical prototypes to AI-enabled successors, it covers cognitive radio (spectrum sensing, dynamic access, handoff, and O-RAN control) and physical-layer cybersecurity (jamming, spoofing, and eavesdropping detection and mitigation), while remaining honest about open problems such as wideband real-time scanning, robustness to correlated noise, and the recurring cost of retraining. Together, the two surveys offer a practical reference for moving from abstract AI ideas toward reproducible, hardware-grounded wireless systems.

  1. S. R. Doha and A. Abdelhadi, “Deep Learning in Wireless Communication Receivers: A Survey,” IEEE Access 2025. (IEEE Xplore)

  2. S. R. Doha and A. Abdelhadi, “Artificial Intelligence in Software Defined Radio: A Survey,” IEEE Access 2025. (IEEE Xplore)

Modulation Classification Resiliency Against Adversarial Attacks using Mixture GAN

 

In this project, we propose a novel generative adversarial network (GAN)-based countermeasure approach to safeguard the Deep Neural Network (DNN)-based Automatic modulation classification (AMC) systems against adversarial attack examples. GAN-based aims to eliminate the the adversarial attack examples before feeding to the DNN-based classifier. Specifically, we have shown the resiliency of our proposed defense GAN against the Fast-Gradient Sign method (FGSM) algorithm as one of the most powerful kind of attack algorithms to craft the perturbed signals. The existing defense-GAN has been designed for image classification and does not work in our case where the above-mentioned communication system is considered. Thus, our proposed countermeasure approach deploys GANs with a mixture of generators to overcome the mode collapsing problem in typical GAN facing with radio signal classification problem. Simulation results show the effectiveness of our proposed defense GAN so that it could enhance the accuracy of the DNN-based AMC under adversarial attacks to 81%, approximately.

  1. E. Shtaiwi, A. El Ouadrhiri, M. Moradikia, S. Sultana, A. Abdelhadi and Z. Han, Mixture GAN For Modulation Classification Resiliency Against Adversarial Attacks, IEEE GLOBECOM 2022. (IEEE Xplore)

Channel Estimation for RIS-assisted MIMO systems

 

Reconfigurable Intelligent Surface (RIS) has been recently proposed as one of the enabling technologies for Future wireless systems, i.e., Beyond 5G and 6G. RISs provides a new degree of freedom (DoF) that further improves the performance of wireless systems. RISs can smartly shape the propagation environment by changing the phase shifts of its passive elements. The optimal control of the RIS requires the availability of Channel State Information (CSI). In this research, we consider the problem of Channel Estimation (CE) for RIS-assisted Multi-User Multiple-Input Multiple-Output (MU-MIMO) systems. The CE process in a RIS-assisted MU-MIMO is challenging since transmitting and/or signal processing means being unworkable at the RIS. Moreover, both the communication and computation overheads increase as the size of RIS elements increase. We investigate the CE problem for an Uplink (UL) channel for a RIS-aided MU-MIMO system. In particular, we propose an algorithm to estimate the composite channel, the separate RIS-based channels, and the direct channel for the RIS-assisted system by exploiting symmetric positive definite (SPD) properties matrices; e.g., invertibility and existence of global minimum, and the uniqueness of the Chelosky decomposition. We divide the entire RIS surface into smaller sub-RISs, and by controlling the changes in these phase shifts for every sub-RIS surface, we can estimate the overall channel. Moreover, we propose a simple passive pilot sequence scheduling scheme and jointly adjusting the phase shift coefficients of elements on each sub-RIS unit. Simulation results provide the Normalized Mean-square Error (NMSE) performance of the proposed approach compared to other CE techniques.

  1. E. Shtaiwi, H. Zhang, S. Vishwanath, M. Youssef, A. Abdelhadi and Z. Han, Channel Estimation Approach for RIS Assisted MIMO Systems, in IEEE Transactions on Cognitive Communications and Networking 2021. (IEEE Xplore)

  2. E. Shtaiwi, H. Zhang, A. Abdelhadi and Z. Han, RIS-Assisted mmWave Channel Estimation Using Convolutional Neural Networks, IEEE Wireless Communications and Networking Conference Workshops (WCNCW) 2021. (IEEE Xplore)

Application of Differential Privacy in Deep Learning

 

In the last few years, differential privacy (DP) has become a de facto standard for protecting the privacy of clients in deep learning. There are many recent research works based on differential privacy to protect the privacy in deep learning. We analyze and present the main ideas based on DP to protect users’ privacy in deep learning and federated learning. We provide an illustration of all types of random noises that satisfy the DP privacy mechanism, with their properties and use cases. Our study reveals the gap between theory and application, accuracy and robustness of DP, which brings forth many future research directions. Additionally, we propose a methodology for continual learning with a tradeoff between privacy and utility and mitigation of catastrophic forgetting.

RIS-assisted Radar and Communication Coexistence System

 

Next-generation wireless communication systems are believed to share the same spectrum previously allocated to radar applications. The coexisting communication system will cause harmful interference to the radar system. In this paper, we investigate the deployment of the Reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS) to improve the performance of a Multiple-Input Multiple-Output (MIMO) Radar and Communication Coexistence (RCC) system. RIS consists of a large number of nearly passive, and low-cost cost elements. RIS provides passive, and a relatively high beamforming gain by controlling the reflecting elements’ reflection coefficients. Moreover, RIS can eliminate the mutual interference between the radar and communication systems. Meanwhile, we optimally design the transmit beamforming and the phase shifts for the RIS elements to improve the achievable sum-rate performance for the communication system. We jointly optimize the phase shifts and the beamforming vectors by using the local search approach. Numerical results verify the effectiveness of the utilization of the RIS to enhance the downlink sum-rate for the communication system users without interrupting the radar operation.

  1. E. Shtaiwi, H. Zhang, A. Abdelhadi and Z. Han, Sum-rate Maximization for RIS-assisted Radar and Communication Coexistence System, IEEE GLOBECOM 2021. (IEEE Xplore)

Physical Layer Security of Multiple Access Networks

 

The demand for high data rate and high reliability in 5G and beyond 5G (B5G) is drastically increasing upon user density increasing. Thus, to concurrently serve multiple user, having different services, at the same frequency or time resource, necessity of a multiple access (MA) technique comes into prominence. Recently rate splitting (RS) multiple access (RSMA), which is a generalized joint framework of non orthogonal-multipleaccess (NOMA) and space-division-multiple access (SDMA), was introduced as a promising MA transmission scheme. Meanwhile, physical (PHY) layer security (PLS) has been extensively regarded as a promising approach against security threats. In this research work, we investigate the PLS designs on MA networks with the emphasis on RSMA technique. While using the RSMA, we adopt novel PHY technologies, e.g., reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS) and UAV, with the aim of improving the secrecy objective. On the other hand, with the proliferation of mobile users increases both the eavesdropping probability of users’ messages and the energy consumption. We consider a UAV-aided cellular network including an over-sailing macro BS and several UAV-aided cells as well as macro-cell users. Multi-antenna UAVs aim to communicate with two legitimate users in the presence of Eve. Given the limited power budget, to strike a tradeoff between the achievable secrecy rate and the energy consumption, we propose energy efficient secure communications in MA networks by appropriately joint design of the RSMA precoder and resource allocation. Our simulation results have shown that the proposed strategy outperforms both the NOMA and the SDMA strategies

Security of Power Systems

 

Smart grid is built by combination of electric and information technologies and achieves the two-way interaction between power utilization and power generation. Unfortunately, a new security threat appears together with cyber-physical communication systems. In order to properly monitor power network, an effective cyber attack detection and state estimation method are required to know attack and system states. This article considers the problem of robust grid state estimation and suggests a technique for distributed state estimation in power networks. First, the distribution power system incorporating multiple synchronous generators is modeled as a state-space framework, where attack occurs in measurements. Basically, the false data injection attacks can interfere with state estimation process by tampering with sensor measurements. Using mean squared error principle, the distributed dynamic state estimation algorithm is designed where local and neighboring gains are obtained using optimal filter and graph theory. For local gain computation, the attack parameter is obtained using the Bayesian learning process. The convergence condition of the proposed approach is derived. Extensive simulation results show that the proposed approach is able to estimate the system state within a short period of time. Hopefully, the proposed methodology can be used to tolerate the cyber attacks for improving the confidence of the grid state estimation process.

Fair Deep Learning models

 

Currently, deep learning models may take unfair decisions due to bias in the training datasets. Often, the bias is discrimination due to sensitive attributes such as gender, race, or ethnicity. In fact, discrimination is a daily reality that continues to exist in many fields, e.g. hiring processes, workplaces. In this paper, we empirically prove that learning models trained on biased datasets will produce unfair and discriminating models. To handle this problem, we propose a pre-processing approach based on differential privacy. Specifically, we implement the randomized response mechanism on the sensitive attribute to mitigate the inequity and avoid discrimination from the training dataset. We evaluate our approach in a hiring process using a synthetic dataset of resumes of candidates. Simulation results show that our approach mitigates bias and takes more fair decisions compared to the TensorFlow differential privacy library or a learning model without our pre-processing approach.

  1. A. El Ouadrhiri, A. Abdelhadi, Differential Privacy for Fair Deep Learning Models, IEEE SysCon 2021. (IEEE Xplore)

Security and Privacy in Smart Grids

 

We introduce a secure energy trading auction approach to schedule the power plant limited resources during peak hours time slots. In the proposed auction model, the power plant serving a power grid shares with the smart meters its maximum available resources during the next future peak time slot; smart meters (SM)s expecting a demand for additional power during future peak hours participate in the power auction by submitting bids of their offered price for their requested amount of power. To protect bidders’ privacy, homomorphic encryption is used to secure their bidding values and ensure avoiding bidrigging and frauds by the auctioneer or other consumers. We propose an effective power scheduling mechanism to distribute the operator's limited resources among smart meters participating in the power auction. Finally, we present simulation results for the performance of our secure power scheduling auction mechanism.

Machine to Machine Communications: Optimality

 

We study the delay-performance for a generic Machine to Machine (M2M) uplink from the sensors to a Central Controller (CC). The uplink traffic is broadly classified as either Periodic Update (PU) and Event Driven (ED). The PU arrivals from different sensors are periodic whereas the ED arrivals are random and typically have low-arrival rate. The latency requirements of PU and ED packets differ greatly based on the criticality and importance of the M2M application. Our goal is to maximize the overall system utility while being proportionally fair to both PU and ED data. We show novel low-complexity packet schedulers (efficient and optimal) by determining the efficient/optimal fraction of time each of the PU and ED packets are served with (preemptive) priority. Using simulations for the queuing process at CC, we verify the correctness of the analytical result for our designed schedulers and compare their performance with various state-of-the-art scheduling policies such as First-Come First-Served (FCFS), (preemptive) priority policy. We show that the proposed optimal scheduler performs better than the existing schedulers for various simulation scenarios.

Machine to Machine Communications: Security

 

Machine-to-Machine (M2M) networks being connected to the internet at large, inherit all the cyber-vulnerabilities of the standard Information Technology (IT) systems. Since perfect cyber-security and robustness is an idealistic construct, it is worthwhile to design intrusion detection schemes to quickly detect and mitigate the harmful consequences of cyber-attacks. Volumetric anomaly detection have been popularized due to their low-complexity, but they cannot detect low-volume sophisticated attacks and also suffer from high false-alarm rate. To over-come these limitations, feature-based detection schemes have been studied for IT networks. However these schemes cannot be easily adapted to M2M systems due to the fundamental architectural and functional differences between the M2M and IT systems. In this paper, we propose novel feature-based detection schemes for a general M2M uplink to detect distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS) attacks, emergency scenarios and terminal device failures. The detection for DDoS attack and emergency senarios involves building up a database of legitimate M2M connections during a training phase and then flagging the new M2M connections as anomalies during the evaluation phase. To distinguish between DDoS attack and emergency scenarios that yield similar signatures for anomaly detection schemes, we propose a modified Canberra distance metric. It basically measures the similarity or differences in the characteristics of inter-arrival time epochs for any two anomalous streams. We investigated alignment schemes for multicast traffic over an equal path length multihop time-varying circularly symmetric fading channels. In another research work, we investigated the impact of mobility on the capacity scaling laws for wireless multicast networks. The following summarizes our main contributions: (i). analogous to the beneficial impact that mobility has on the throughput of unicast networks, we establish that mobility can provide a similar gain in the order-wise growth-rate of the throughput for multicast networks.
